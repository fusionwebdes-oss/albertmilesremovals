      <h2>The 18 Tips</h2>
      <ol>
        <li><strong>Never pack a heavy item in a big box.</strong> Books and plates go in small boxes — big ones are for light, bulky things like bedding.</li>
        <li><strong>Keep boxes under 20kg.</strong> That's the safe carrying weight for a mover. Heavy boxes slow everyone down and get damaged.</li>
        <li><strong>Tape the bottom of every box</strong> with a "H" of tape — two strips across and one along. Dozens of boxes fail at the bottom fold.</li>
        <li><strong>Use wadding and paper, not just bubble wrap.</strong> Newspaper ink transfers; acid-free paper protects china and glass better.</li>
        <li><strong>Plates travel on edge</strong>, like records — not flat. Stack them upright, separated by paper.</li>
        <li><strong>Fill every gap.</strong> A box that moves inside a box is how things break. Use tea towels and clothes as free void-fillers.</li>
        <li><strong>Label by room, not by list.</strong> "Kitchen — one of three" tells the crew exactly where to put it.</li>
        <li><strong>Label the side, not the top.</strong> Boxes are carried from the sides — labels on top get covered by the box above.</li>
        <li><strong>Keep an essentials box for each overnight.</strong> Kettle, mugs, teabags, loo roll, chargers, meds, a change of clothes.</li>
        <li><strong>Pack the bed last and unpack it first.</strong> You'll thank yourself at 10pm.</li>
        <li><strong>Use wardrobe boxes for hanging clothes.</strong> It saves the ironing marathon after a big move.</li>
        <li><strong>Take drawers out for a heavy chest</strong> — or remove the contents and tape the drawers shut. The piece becomes half the weight.</li>
        <li><strong>Wrap mirrors and artwork in blankets</strong> face-out and tape the corners — never the glass surface.</li>
        <li><strong>Put screws and fittings in a labelled bag</strong> taped to the item they came from. No mystery hardware at the new house.</li>
        <li><strong>Photograph electronics wiring</strong> before unplugging. Reassembling the TV cabinet becomes a five-minute job.</li>
        <li><strong>Pack a small "open first" box</strong> with tools, light bulbs, and the kettle — for the new address.</li>
        <li><strong>Don't pack the kettle for the last morning</strong> — it's the single most-repacked item in the country.</li>
        <li><strong>Consider professional packing.</strong> It costs less than you think and takes the whole job off your plate — see our <a href="../services/packing-services.html">packing service</a>.</li>
      </ol>
